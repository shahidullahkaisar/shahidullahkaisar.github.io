About

From the lab bench to the wavefunction.

I'm a physics major turned materials researcher. My work sits at the meeting point of experiment and computation: in the lab, I use laser-based techniques like the Z-scan to measure how materials respond to intense light; on the computer, I use density functional theory to understand why they respond that way.

My master's thesis investigates the nonlinear optical responses of black tea grades using the Z-scan technique — measuring nonlinear refractive indices of natural, polyphenol-rich materials and exploring their potential in optical power limiting.

Long term, I want to work as a materials scientist designing functional materials — where quantum simulation, machine learning, and careful experiments come together.

Research

Three threads, one goal: understanding materials.

EXPERIMENT · Z-SCAN

Nonlinear optics of natural materials

Measuring nonlinear refractive indices (n₂) of black tea grades (GBOP, DUST, CD) with a Gaussian-beam Z-scan setup — self-focusing, self-defocusing, and optical power limiting behavior in polyphenol-rich solutions.

COMPUTATION · DFT

Density functional theory

First-principles studies of electronic structure — band structures, density of states, and optical properties — to connect measured behavior back to the quantum mechanics of electrons in materials.

FRONTIER · QUANTUM

Quantum computing for materials

Exploring how variational quantum algorithms and quantum simulation could tackle materials problems that are hard for classical computers — the next chapter of computational materials science.

Currently: writing up my MS thesis on the nonlinear optical response of black tea grades, including n₂ hierarchy across grades and saturation behavior at high intensity. Ask me about it — I love talking Z-scan curves.
